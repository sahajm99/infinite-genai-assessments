ASTHMA — CLINICAL REFERENCE (SYNTHETIC / FICTIONAL)

Classification.
Asthma severity ranges from intermittent to mild, moderate, and severe persistent, based
on daytime symptoms, nighttime awakenings, rescue inhaler use, and lung function.

Reliever therapy.
Short-acting beta-2 agonists (SABA) such as albuterol provide rapid relief of acute
bronchospasm. Current guidance also supports as-needed low-dose inhaled corticosteroid
combined with formoterol as reliever therapy in adults and adolescents.

Controller therapy.
Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) are the cornerstone of long-term controller therapy for
persistent asthma. Step-up options include combining an ICS with a long-acting beta-2
agonist (LABA). A LABA should never be used as monotherapy in asthma.

Acute exacerbation.
Treatment of an acute asthma exacerbation includes repeated SABA, systemic
corticosteroids, and supplemental oxygen to maintain oxygen saturation of 93-95%.
