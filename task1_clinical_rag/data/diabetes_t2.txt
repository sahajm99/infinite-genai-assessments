TYPE 2 DIABETES — CLINICAL REFERENCE (SYNTHETIC / FICTIONAL)

Diagnosis.
Type 2 diabetes is diagnosed by any of: fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher,
a 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test,
an HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, or a random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher with classic
symptoms. Prediabetes corresponds to an HbA1c of 5.7% to 6.4%.

First-line therapy.
Metformin is the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for type 2 diabetes, combined with
lifestyle modification. Metformin is contraindicated when the eGFR is below 30 mL/min.

Glycemic targets.
A reasonable HbA1c goal for many non-pregnant adults is below 7.0%. Less stringent goals
(for example below 8.0%) may be appropriate for older adults or those with limited life
expectancy or significant comorbidities.

Agents with cardiovascular and renal benefit.
In patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney
disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor or a GLP-1 receptor agonist is recommended in addition to
metformin, independent of the HbA1c level.
