HYPERTENSION — CLINICAL REFERENCE (SYNTHETIC / FICTIONAL)

Definition and staging.
Normal blood pressure is below 120/80 mmHg. Elevated blood pressure is a systolic
reading of 120-129 mmHg with a diastolic below 80 mmHg. Stage 1 hypertension is
130-139 systolic or 80-89 diastolic. Stage 2 hypertension is 140/90 mmHg or higher.

First-line pharmacologic therapy.
For most adults with stage 1 hypertension who require medication, a thiazide diuretic
(for example chlorthalidone) is a recommended first-line agent. Other acceptable
first-line options include ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and
calcium channel blockers. Beta-blockers are not preferred as initial monotherapy unless
there is a compelling indication such as heart failure or prior myocardial infarction.

Lifestyle measures.
Sodium restriction, the DASH dietary pattern, weight loss, regular aerobic exercise, and
limiting alcohol are first steps and are recommended for all patients regardless of stage.

Monitoring.
After starting or adjusting therapy, blood pressure should be rechecked in 2-4 weeks.
Basic metabolic panel and potassium should be checked within 2-4 weeks of starting an
ACE inhibitor, ARB, or diuretic.
